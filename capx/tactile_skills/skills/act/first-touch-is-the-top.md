## How

A ball is a sphere: its top is one radius above its centre.  A descent that
stops on the first pad contact has met that top, and a close made there pinches
the upper cap rather than holding the ball.

Descend to the ball's own mid-height before closing.  The pads' centre sits
0.118 m below the wrist in this scene, so with a resting ball's centre at
z = 0.050 m the wrist that puts the pads on the centre is about 0.168 m.  The
first contact will come before that; it is a landmark, not the seat.

## Traps

- The first contact is the top even when the ball sits a few millimetres off
  the row line; the descent knows the height, not the lateral error.
- A close at the top can still press the gel, so the reading does not look
  empty; the ball comes out on the first lift ([[search-continues-after-miss]]).
